DETAILED JUSTIFICATIONS
============================================================

--- annoyance ---
  Current mapping : angry
  Action          : KEEP
  Justification   : GoEmotions defines annoyance as 'mild irritation'. Sample texts confirm irritation language ('This is so frustrating', 'That bugs me'). Annoyance is a lower-intensity anger variant. The Angry cluster intentionally groups anger-family emotions. Mapping is semantically sound.

--- approval ---
  Current mapping : happy
  Action          : KEEP
  Justification   : GoEmotions defines approval as 'favorable opinion'. Sample texts show positive sentiment ('That's a great idea', 'Well done'). While not identical to joy, approval consistently carries positive valence that aligns with Happy. Mapping is directionally correct.

--- confusion ---
  Current mapping : neutral
  Action          : REMOVE
  Justification   : GoEmotions defines confusion as 'a state of being bewildered or unclear'. Sample texts show uncertainty ('I don't understand why this happened') which lacks the emotional valence needed for Neutral. Neutral should be emotionally flat, not cognitively uncertain. Discarding avoids contaminating Neutral with non-emotional cognitive states.

--- disapproval ---
  Current mapping : angry
  Action          : KEEP
  Justification   : GoEmotions defines disapproval as 'unfavorable opinion'. Sample texts show negative judgment ('That's wrong', 'I disagree with this approach'). While milder than anger, disapproval consistently carries negative valence aligned with the Angry cluster. Acceptable mapping.

--- disgust ---
  Current mapping : angry
  Action          : KEEP
  Justification   : GoEmotions defines disgust as 'strong aversion'. Sample texts show revulsion ('That's disgusting', 'I can't stand it'). Disgust is a negative rejection emotion that aligns with the Angry cluster's 'rejection/disapproval' theme. Mapping is acceptable, though disgust could arguably be its own category in a finer-grained system.

--- embarrassment ---
  Current mapping : sad
  Action          : KEEP
  Justification   : GoEmotions defines embarrassment as 'self-conscious discomfort'. Sample texts show shame-adjacent language ('I can't believe I did that', 'So humiliating'). Embarrassment is a self-directed negative emotion that aligns with Sad. While mild, the valence is consistently negative. Acceptable mapping.

--- fear ---
  Current mapping : sad
  Action          : KEEP
  Justification   : GoEmotions defines fear as 'anticipation of danger'. Sample texts show anxiety and dread ('I'm scared something bad will happen', 'That terrifies me'). Fear is a strongly negative emotion. While distinct from sadness, our 4-class system groups all negative non-anger emotions under Sad. Mapping is consistent with the schema.

--- realization ---
  Current mapping : NONE (not mapped)
  Action          : REMOVE
  Justification   : GoEmotions defines realization as 'sudden understanding or awareness'. This is a cognitive state, not an emotion. It has no natural mapping to any of the 4 target emotions. Samples are already being discarded by prepare_datasets.py since it is absent from GOEMOTIONS_MAP. Confirming removal.

--- remorse ---
  Current mapping : sad
  Action          : KEEP
  Justification   : GoEmotions defines remorse as 'deep regret for wrongdoing'. Sample texts show genuine sadness/regret ('I wish I hadn't done that', 'I feel terrible about it'). Remorse maps cleanly to the Sad cluster as a guilt-related sadness. Mapping is correct.

--- surprise ---
  Current mapping : neutral
  Action          : REMOVE
  Justification   : GoEmotions defines surprise as 'sudden feeling of wonder'. Surprise can be positive (happy surprise) or negative (shock). Mapping to Neutral forces a valence-free label onto a potentially valenced emotion. Sample texts include both positive and negative surprises. Discarding rather than misclassifying.
